## Slide 03 — What Is Phishing?
**Title:** WHAT IS PHISHING?  
**Subtitle:** Understanding the threat

**Definition:**  
Phishing is a type of social engineering attack where cybercriminals impersonate legitimate organizations to trick victims into revealing sensitive information, installing malware, or transferring money.

**Statistics:**
- 91% — of cyberattacks start with phishing
- $4.9B — lost to phishing scams in 2023
- 3.4B — phishing emails sent daily worldwide

**Note:** The term "phishing" comes from "fishing" — attackers cast bait hoping someone will bite.

## Slide 05 — Types of Phishing
**Title:** TYPES OF PHISHING ATTACKS  
**Subtitle:** Know your enemy

| Type | Delivery | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| EMAIL PHISHING | Email | Mass emails impersonating legitimate companies. Most common form. Uses generic greetings and urgent language. |
| SPEAR PHISHING | Email | Targeted at specific individuals using personal info. Highly convincing. Used in corporate espionage. |
| WHALING | Email | Attacks targeting C-level executives (CEOs, CFOs). High-value targets for maximum damage. |
| SMISHING | SMS | Phishing via SMS text messages. Exploits mobile trust and lower vigilance. |
| VISHING | Phone | Voice phishing via phone calls. Attackers pose as bank or IT support. |

## Slide 07 — Anatomy of a Phishing Email
**Title:** ANATOMY OF A PHISHING EMAIL  
**Subtitle:** Red flags to watch for

**Email Mockup:**
- From: security-alert@paypall-verify.com ❌
- To: you@yourmail.com
- Subject: ⚠️ URGENT: Your account will be SUSPENDED!

**Body:**
> Dear Valued Customer,  
> We have detected suspicious activity on your PayPal account. You must verify your account IMMEDIATELY or it will be permanently closed within 24 hours.  
> [ VERIFY MY ACCOUNT NOW ]  
> PayPal Security Team

**Red Flags Identified:**
1. ❌ Misspelled sender domain: 'paypall-verify.com'
2. ❌ Urgent language designed to trigger panic
3. ❌ Generic greeting — no real name used
4. ❌ Suspicious CTA button leading to fake site
5. ❌ Artificial 24-hour deadline creates pressure
6. ✅ Hover any link before clicking — ALWAYS

## Slide 08 — Fake Website Detection
**Title:** FAKE WEBSITE DETECTION  
**Subtitle:** How to spot spoofed pages

**URL Comparison:**
| Real URL | Fake URL |
|----------|----------|
| https://paypal.com | https://paypal-secure.verify-login.com |
| https://microsoft.com | https://mlcrosoft.com/signin |
| https://bankofamerica.com | https://bankofamerica.security-check.net |

**How to Verify a Website:**
- ✅ Padlock icon (HTTPS) — necessary but not sufficient!
- ❌ Extra words in domain: 'paypal-secure.com' is NOT PayPal
- ❌ Typosquatting: 'mlcrosoft' instead of 'microsoft'
- 🔍 Check the exact domain — everything before the first '/'
- ✅ Bookmark important sites — never click email links
- ✅ Use WHOIS lookup to check domain registration date

## Slide 09 — Social Engineering Tactics
**Title:** SOCIAL ENGINEERING TACTICS  
**Subtitle:** Manipulation techniques attackers use

| Tactic | Example | Mechanism |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| URGENCY | "Your account will be DELETED in 2 hours!" | Creates panic, bypasses rational thinking |
| AUTHORITY | "This is your bank manager / IT department." | Impersonates trusted figures to gain compliance |
| FEAR | "Police have flagged your account for fraud." | Triggers fear response, clouds judgment |
| SCARCITY | "Only 1 spot left — claim your prize NOW!" | FOMO drives rash decisions |
| RECIPROCITY | "We sent you a gift. Just confirm your info." | Exploits feeling obliged to return a favor |
| SOCIAL PROOF | "10,000 users already verified — join them!" | 'Everyone else is doing it' pressure tactic |

## Slide 10 — Psychology of Phishing
**Title:** HOW ATTACKERS HACK YOUR BRAIN  
**Subtitle:** The psychology of phishing

**Left Panel:**
- System 1 thinking: We react instinctively to danger cues without analysis
- Cognitive overload: Busy people make worse security decisions
- Trust heuristics: Familiar logos trigger automatic trust

**Right Panel:**
- Confirmation bias: We see what we expect to see
- Authority bias: We obey authority figures without questioning
- Loss aversion: Fear of losing something > desire to gain

**Defense:** Always PAUSE before clicking. Apply the '10-second rule' — stop, breathe, verify the sender and URL before any action.

## Slide 12 — Credential Theft
**Title:** CREDENTIAL THEFT EXPLAINED  
**Subtitle:** How your passwords get stolen

**Attack Flow:**
Phishing Email Arrives → Victim Clicks Fake Link → Fake Login Page Loads → Credentials Submitted → Attacker Gains Access

**Theft Methods:**
- **Credential Harvesting** — Fake login pages collect username & password and redirect to real site. Victim never knows.
- **Man-in-the-Browser** — Malware intercepts browser sessions, stealing session tokens to bypass login entirely.
- **Credential Stuffing** — Stolen credentials tested on 100s of sites automatically. Reused passwords = multiple breaches.
- **Account Takeover (ATO)** — Attacker changes password, locks you out, uses account for fraud, or sells access on dark web.

## Slide 13 — Multi-Factor Authentication
**Title:** MULTI-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION (MFA)  
**Subtitle:** Your best defense against credential theft

**Key Message:** Even if attackers steal your password, MFA stops them cold.

**Three Factors:**
1. Something You KNOW — Password, PIN, security question
2. Something You HAVE — Phone OTP, authenticator app, hardware key
3. Something You ARE — Fingerprint, face ID, retina scan

**MFA Best Practices:**
- Use an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy) — NOT SMS where possible
- Hardware keys (YubiKey) are the strongest MFA — phishing-resistant by design
- Never share your OTP with anyone — banks and tech companies NEVER ask for it
- Enable MFA on: email, banking, social media, cloud storage — all critical accounts

## Slide 14 — Password Hygiene
**Title:** PASSWORD HYGIENE  
**Subtitle:** Strong passwords = stronger security

**DO THIS:**
- Use 12+ character passwords
- Use passphrase: 'Coffee!Rain#Book9'
- Use a password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password)
- Use unique password for every account
- Change passwords after any breach

**NEVER DO THIS:**
- Use 'password123' or 'qwerty'
- Use your name, birthday, or pet's name
- Reuse passwords across websites
- Store passwords in a text file or browser
- Share passwords via email or chat

## Slide 15 — Safe Browsing & Reporting
**Title:** SAFE BROWSING & REPORTING  
**Subtitle:** Your daily security checklist

| Practice | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Verify URLs | Always check the full URL before entering credentials. Look for https and the exact domain name. |
| Hover Before Click | Hover over any link to preview the actual destination URL shown in your browser's status bar. |
| Use Security Tools | Install browser extensions: uBlock Origin, HTTPS Everywhere, Malwarebytes Browser Guard. |
| Report Suspicious Emails | Use the 'Report Phishing' button in Gmail/Outlook. Forward to reportphishing@apwg.org. |
| Keep Software Updated | OS and browser patches fix security vulnerabilities. Enable auto-updates. |
| Use VPN on Public WiFi | Public WiFi is prime hunting ground. Use a trusted VPN to encrypt your traffic. |

## Slide 18 — Prevention Playbook
**Title:** YOUR PHISHING DEFENSE PLAYBOOK  
**Subtitle:** 10 rules to live by

1. Never click links in unexpected emails — go directly to the website
2. Verify sender email addresses — look beyond the display name
3. Enable MFA on all critical accounts — no exceptions
4. Use a password manager — unique strong password per site
5. Hover over links before clicking — check actual destination
6. Never enter credentials on a site you reached via email
7. Report phishing — help protect your entire organization
8. When in doubt, call the organization directly using official number
9. Keep software and OS updated — patch known vulnerabilities
10. Train regularly — attackers evolve, so should your knowledge
